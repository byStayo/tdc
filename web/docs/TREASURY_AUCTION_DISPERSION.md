# Treasury competitive auction spreads

This private events-family study asks whether an unusually wide spread between the highest and median accepted yields predicts a softer TLT regular session after accounting for normal auction demand. Treasury defines the median by the dollar amount of accepted competitive bids. The control is the reported bid-to-cover ratio. The two inputs come from the same auction and Treasury is the producer of all three deliveries. [Treasury auction data and field definitions](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/treasury-securities-auctions-data/treasury-securities-auctions-data-updated/) · [Treasury competitive results](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/announcements-data-results/announcement-results-press-releases/auction-results/)

The fixed population is nominal 2-, 3-, 5-, 7-, 10-, 20- and 30-year notes and bonds. Each auction is compared with its original tenor in each of the preceding three calendar months, including reopenings. The signed candidate is the negative change in the high-minus-median yield spread from the median of those three spreads, in basis points. The control is the change from the median of the same three bid-to-cover ratios. Multiple nominal auctions sharing a target date, an absent reference month or an unresolved exchange-calendar gap makes that target ineligible.

## Publisher operation

From the publishing host’s own `web` directory, create the single immutable registration with `npm run consensus:auction-dispersion-study -- --register`. The optional `--capture` command obtains a Treasury-only source panel on the current clock. It records the native source only. Use `--status` to inspect actual counts.

The ordinary calendar collection uses the same announcement catalogue that the public forecast cites. Once registered, it matches the four selected records against the TreasuryDirect auctioned catalogue and each original competitive results XML. It verifies the auction time, security identity, units and reported fields, and retains dated original bytes under `.consensus/private-treasury-award-dispersion/`. The HTTP modification timestamp dates the native document. The later actual receipt proves the data had reached the publisher. Legacy XML can also supply an explicit release minute. Each candidate edition is bound to the exact announcement-catalogue hash and receipt time and the first verified source panel. Newly corrected economic values cannot overwrite it.

The next reviewed 390-minute session is the only permitted target. Holidays and documented short sessions are skipped. The normal pre-open verifier must witness the final qualifying edition on that morning, and the primary settlement transaction must subsequently receive an eligible TLT daily outcome for that same edition. The host must have registered before its original panel acquisitions and the panel and matching edition source must be sealed before the issue cutoff. An isolated QA run cannot supply host enrollment or confer permission to backdate it.

The first 300 eligible training, next 100 eligible validation, and next 100 eligible reserved test decisions retain the common chronological rules and existing holdouts. Compare the fitted candidate to the matched control, training prevalence and 0.50 under the registered multiplicity and Brier gates. The Treasury schedule remains an unsigned public observation. The experiment measures the distribution of competitive bids. [Frozen policy](../lib/consensus/auction-dispersion-policy.ts) · [First-settlement custody](../lib/consensus/secondary-study.ts)
